### Scratch Notes:
“In this report, we combine resting state functional connectivity MRI (rs-fcMRI), graph analysis, community detection, and spring-embedding visualization techniques to analyze four separate networks defined in earlier studies.” (those being the default mode network, cingulo-opercular network, fronto-parietal network, and the cerebellar network). They find a developmental trend changing functional organization from proximal association to functional association: “ Communities in children are predominantly arranged by anatomical proximity, while communities in adults predominantly reflect functional relationships, as defined from adult fMRI studies.” This is the ‘local to distributed’ organization described in the title. 
Contrary to the previous paper, this paper asserts the validity of discrete modularity of cortex in distinct types of information processing, but states that they are leveraged by different functional networks for cognition. 
“Resting state fcMRI is based on the discovery that spontaneous low-frequency (,,0.1 Hz) blood oxygen level dependent (BOLD) signal fluctuations in sometimes distant, but functionally-related grey matter regions, show strong correlations at rest” - IE functionally similar grey matter regions correlate when at rest. This finding has been used in examining motor, memory, attention, and task control systems.
The different networks have different ‘profiles’, like activating on separate time scales.
Spring embedding visualization showed the local tendencies of child networks, compared to the adults’.
Ultimate finding in author summary: “We find that brain regions in children communicate with other regions more locally but that over age communication becomes more distributed. Interestingly, the efficiency of communication in children (measured as a ‘small world’ network) is comparable to that of adults. We argue that these findings have important implications for understanding both the maturation and the function of neural systems in typical and atypical development”
