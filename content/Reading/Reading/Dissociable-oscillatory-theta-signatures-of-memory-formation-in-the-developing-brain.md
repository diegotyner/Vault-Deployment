### Scratch Notes:
This paper is reporting on results of a specially crafted memory task in order to attempt to find trends in the neural activity of successful and unsuccessful encoded memories. They find that certain oscillatory frequencies (fast (7Hz) vs slow (3Hz) theta frequencies) show both age related and performance related effects. Also important, interplay between the medial temporal lobe (MTL) and the prefrontal cortex (PFC).  
Previous research shows the medial temporal lobe is central to episodic memory and memory formation, while the prefrontal cortex is associated with generally improved memory. The PFC is one of the latest developing areas while the MTL generally improves earlier, so theories accounting for memory performance from children to adulthood also involve the PFC. Coactivation between them also shows performance gains: “Performance gains are thus posited to result from a maturing dialog between MTL and PFC” although mechanism unknown. This paper attempts to address this gap, and investigates theta band activity specifically.  
They do this through invasive data recorded from epilepsy patients (standard procedure), analyzing electrocorticography (ECoG) data. This data has a number of advantages, allowing them to perform stronger analyses (better spatial and temporal resolution, as well as a higher signal to noise ratio).  
Their results showed:  
1) Linear improvement in performance with age  
2) strong evidence of systematic activity of the fast and slow theta oscillations coordinating activity between the MTL and PFC,  
3) change in mean frequency centers with age (slow theta decreases with age, fast theta increases).
